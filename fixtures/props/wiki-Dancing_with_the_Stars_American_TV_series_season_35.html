Season 34

Season thirty-five of Dancing with the Stars will premiere on ABC and Disney+ on September 15, 2026.[1] This season will be the fourth to air live on both networks simultaneously. Season nineteen champion Alfonso Ribeiro will return to host the installment, while former professional dancer and judge Julianne Hough will return as co-host.[2]

Cast

Couples

On April 22, 2026, during an upfront for Hulu's unscripted programming, Ciara Miller and Maura Higgins were announced as the first celebrity participants for the thirty-fifth season.[3] Jackson Olson joined the cast three weeks later during an upfront for Disney's original programming.[4] On June 17, TMZ reported that Guillermo Rodriguez joined the cast;[5] Jimmy Kimmel confirmed his appearance later that day.[6] Throughout August, Tyler Cameron, Jenna Dewan,[7] Julia Stiles, Tatyana Ali,[8] Sarah Jane Nader and Conner Leavitt were named to the cast.[9] Nader is the younger sister of season thirty-three contestant Brooks Nader, and Leavitt is the husband of season thirty-four semi-finalist Whitney Leavitt.[10] Amber Glenn and Ezra Frech became the first Olympic and Paralympic champions to compete during the same season.[11]

The first celebrity-professional dancer pairing, Rodriguez and the reigning champion Witney Carson, was announced on Good Morning America on August 17, 2026.[12] At the time of the announcement, Carson was four months pregnant with her third child.[13] She will become the first pro to enter an installment of Dancing with the Stars while pregnant,[14] and the first to compete that far into her second trimester.[13] Gleb Savchenko did not return as a pro this season.[15] After a five-year hiatus, Sharna Burgess returned to the lineup on August 24.[16] Adele Zaikman, winner of Dancing with the Stars: The Next Pro, joined the series as a first-time pro on August 31.[17] She is the first Israeli professional dancer to be featured on the series.[18] Season thirty-four troupe member Hailey Bills was promoted to a first-time pro.[19] Her pairing with Nader marked the third same-sex partnership in the series' history, following JoJo Siwa & Jenna Johnson from season thirty and Shangela & Savchenko from season thirty-one.[20]

The full roster of celebrity participants and their partnerships was revealed on Good Morning America on September 2.[21] With sixteen couples, this season is tied with season nine and season thirty-one as having the largest cast in the show's history.[22]

Hosts and judges

The hosts and judges for the season were announced on Good Morning America on September 1, 2026, one day before the cast reveal.[23] Alfonso Ribeiro and Julianne Hough returned as hosts,[24] while Derek Hough, Carrie Ann Inaba, and Bruno Tonioli returned as judges.[24]

The second season of the Dancing with the Stars podcast, hosted by season thirty-four quarter-finalist Andy Richter and sponsored by the international bakery chain Paris Baguette,[25] was launched on September 10.[26] He revealed the songs and dance styles for the two-night season premiere.[27] An official Dancing with the Stars aftershow, hosted by season thirty-four champion Robert Irwin, will air live immediately after the second night premiere episode on September 16.[26]

Dance troupe

Kamri Peterson, a former pro on Dancing with the Stars: Juniors, was announced as a first-time troupe member on August 2, 2026, during the series' inaugural fan convention.[28] Jaxon Willard, Carter Williams, and Onye Stevenson were announced as second-year troupe members on September 4.[29]

Week 1: Premiere

In a special two-night season premiere, each couple will perform one unlearned dance to a song that topped one of Billboard magazine's music charts during the year each celebrity was born.[31] They divided themselves between men and women; the male celebrities will perform on the first night, and the female celebrities will perform on the second night.[32] One couple will be eliminated at the end of each night.[33]

Co-host Julianne Hough choreographed a number inspired by the season's sponsor, Paris Baguette.[34] It was her first time choreographing a routine for the series since season twenty.[34]
